![Forgiveness on the Horizon](/images/2026-08-30-forgiveness-on-the-horizon.jpg)

Why do I struggle to extend grace towards myself? I seem to have the capacity to understand and forgive others' failings, but I have a hard time applying the same kindness to the woman in the mirror.

I recently read about looking back on past choices with a ' punishing gaze'. This  is absolutely what I've been doing constantly - and it's exhausting. This mindset robs me of my peace, causing a restless remorse and a loop of regrets.

The article said that it's incredibly unfair to judge a past version of yourself using the wisdom born of those exact hardships that have shaped who you are now.

I made past decisions with limited emotional tools and clarity - doing the best with what I knew at that time.

Insight is something that develops gradually, painstakingly earned through the pain and friction of simply living.

How could I reasonably expect young Lizzie to have deep wisdom and understanding?
 
'Expecting your past self to have possessed deep understanding before walking the difficult path completely ignores the universal law of how human consciousness evolves and ripens over time'.

Understanding that I made choices and decisions from a place of inexperience, helps me extend compassion to my past self. 

Very slowly I am coming to understand that instead of carrying the heavy load of regret, acknowledging my past mistakes and extending self forgiveness is actually proof of emotional and spiritual growth.

Self compassion for surviving a difficult journey as a trainee human paves the way for letting go of criticism and judgement.

I haven't quite reached destination forgiveness, but I'm on my way.
